01 / Project Overview

What DealFlow360 does

Three access surfaces — Admin, Sales/Agent, and a dedicated Customer Portal — replace fragmented spreadsheets and quote tools with one system that enforces real business rules in application logic, not the UI.

🛠Admin Dashboard

Configure discount ceilings, upsell rules, warehouse inventory, approval thresholds and subscription plans.

📊Sales / Agent Dashboard

Build quotes, track deal health, evaluate risk, manage orders, invoices, fulfillment and reports.

🤝Customer Portal

Secure, token-based portal where clients review quotes, counter-negotiate, confirm orders and manage their profile — with a deliberately restricted data shape (no margin/risk leakage).

02 / System Architecture

Modular monolith on Next.js

A single Next.js deployment serves the UI, the API route handlers, and — critically — a dedicated Engine Layer that owns all business logic, kept separate from both the transport layer (routes) and the persistence layer (services + Prisma).

Presentation Auth / Middleware Transport (routes) Service layer Engine layer (business rules) Persistence External AI

🧭 The one architectural rule the codebase enforces

Route handlers never contain business logic. Every route delegates to a service, which delegates to one or more pure-function engines. This keeps pricing, discount, margin, risk, and approval calculations unit-testable in isolation and guarantees the customer-portal negotiation flow runs through the exact same pipeline as internal quotes — no "simplified portal version" of the math exists.

03 / Data Flow

Quote lifecycle, end to end

The canonical path a quotation travels — from draft to cash — touching every engine in sequence.

STEP 1

Draft & Price

Sales rep builds a multi-line quote. Pricing Engine resolves server-side base price × qty + tax per line (client-supplied unit price is never trusted).

STEP 2

Discount & Margin Check

Discount Engine resolves the binding ceiling (tier vs. category, whichever is lower) per line. Margin Engine computes margin against revenue (not cost) per line and blended for the quote.

STEP 3

Risk Scoring

Risk Engine blends revenue-weighted discount violations, thin-margin flags (<15%), and multi-warehouse fulfillment risk into a LOW → CRITICAL band.

STEP 4

Approval Routing

Approval Engine maps risk band to a sequential approval chain (Sales Manager → Finance for HIGH/CRITICAL), with a hard-violation floor that closes the "dilute one bad line across many good lines" loophole.

STEP 5

Customer Negotiation

Quote is shared via a tokenized portal link. Customer counter-offers re-enter the identical pricing → discount → margin → risk → approval pipeline. Portal responses strip margin, risk score and approval reasoning before leaving the server.

STEP 6

Confirmation → Order

Quote confirmation auto-creates an Order. Fulfillment Engine greedily allocates stock across warehouses (largest-available-first), recording backorders explicitly rather than silently under-filling.

STEP 7

Billing & Invoicing

Billing Engine splits order lines into ONE_TIME (single invoice) vs. RECURRING (subscription + billing schedule with proration on plan/quantity changes). Invoices move DRAFT → ISSUED → PAID.

STEP 8

Deal Health & Upsell (continuous)

Deal Health Engine scores stall risk, discount anomalies, approval delays and negotiation churn over the deal's lifetime. Upsell Engine ranks co-purchase candidates, hard-filtering anything below a 15% margin floor. AI Explanation Layer narrates all of the above in plain English — it never decides anything itself.

04 / Supported Modules & Features

12 business engines, one contract each

Every engine is a pure function: same input → same output, independently testable, no side effects. Click a tab to inspect a group.

01

Pricing Engine

Resolves subtotal, tax amount and line subtotal server-side per line. Client-supplied prices are never trusted — base price always comes from the product/price-list record.

02

Discount Engine

Computes the binding discount ceiling per line — minimum of customer-tier ceiling and product-category ceiling — and records overage independently per line rather than collapsing to a worst-case.

03

Margin Engine

Margin is always calculated against revenue, not cost. Supports negative margins (deceptively thin/undercut lines) and blended quote-level margin across multiple lines.

04

Risk Engine

Additive scoring: revenue-weighted discount violations + thin-margin flag (<15%) + inventory/warehouse-split risk. Produces LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / CRITICAL bands.

05

Approval Engine

LOW needs no approval; MEDIUM needs Sales Manager; HIGH/CRITICAL need Sales Manager then Finance, sequentially. A hard-violation floor forces at least Sales Manager approval if any single line breaches its ceiling by >5%, even if the blended score looks fine.

06

Fulfillment / Warehouse Allocation Engine

Deterministic greedy allocator: sorts warehouses by available stock descending and fills from the largest first, minimizing shipment count. Remaining unmet demand is recorded as an explicit backorder.

07

Billing Engine

Splits order lines into ONE_TIME vs RECURRING. Recurring lines spin up a Subscription + BillingSchedule. Mid-cycle quantity/plan changes are prorated using a daily-rate formula against the current partial period only.

08

Upsell / Cross-Sell Engine

Ranks candidates by 0.6×co-purchase frequency + 0.25×promotion bonus + 0.15×margin delta, with a non-negotiable pre-filter excluding anything under a 15% margin floor — framed to reps as "customers who bought X also bought Y".

09

Negotiation Engine

Customer counter-offers run through the exact same pricing→discount→margin→risk→approval pipeline as internal quotes. Portal responses are network-level restricted — no margin, internal risk score, or approval reasoning ever leaves the server for DRAFT/PENDING_APPROVAL/UNDER_NEGOTIATION quotes.

10

Deal Health Engine

A separate, time-aware score from Risk: stall/inactivity (+5/7-day block, max 20), discount anomaly vs. rep average (+8), approval delay >24h (+10), multi-warehouse/slipped delivery (+10/+15), negotiation churn beyond the 3rd counter (+5/counter, max 20).

11

Audit & Alerts

Every state transition (approvals, negotiations, fulfillment overrides) is written to an immutable AuditLog, surfaced as in-app Notifications and Alerts.

12

AI Explanation Layer

Sits above every other engine and only translates already-computed structured facts into natural language via the Groq API, with a deterministic sentence-builder fallback if the AI call fails, times out, or returns malformed output — never a single point of failure for the demo.

🔐 Auth & RBAC

JWT sessions, bcrypt hashing, role-based access control, per-route rate limiting, tokenized customer invites & onboarding.

📦 Product Catalog

Categories, products, variants (SKU generation), price lists & price-list items, bulk product actions.

📈 Analytics & Reports

Dashboard summaries, filterable reports, PDF export (pdfkit) and XLS export (exceljs) with bulk invoice actions.

🏬 Warehouses & Inventory

Multi-warehouse inventory records, bulk allocation, manual fulfillment overrides per order.

📝 Note on planning vs. implementation

The team's architecture.md planning doc originally scoped Supabase (Auth + Postgres + Realtime) and shadcn/ui. The shipped build consolidated onto a self-managed PostgreSQL + Prisma + custom JWT/RBAC stack and Groq for AI — same architectural boundaries (routes → services → engines), different infra choices made during execution.
